Try a round

Your turn. Run a session yourself.

This is the app, working, in your browser. You are B, the one who does the work. The first screen asks one question; answer it twice and you are ready. From then on you have one button, Next phase, and every phase change is a handshake: you ask, A says yes or not yet, and you press Go. Only two things ever change a clock: the clock running out, and your Go. When time is up the clock stops and waits for you, however long you are away. A runs on autopilot here, so answers are instant. Use the B, A and Admin tabs in the app's header to answer as A yourself or to watch as Admin. Space does the one thing each screen asks for.

Open the app full screenSame app, its own page: app.html. Add #a or #admin to open another screen.

Nothing on B's screen stops a clock. Next phase only asks; the clock you are in keeps running until you press Go, and Go stops it and starts the next one at the same instant. Check-up, planning and the movement break start on their own when the phase before them ends, then wait, frozen, until you press Go. Close the window and the round carries on in the menu bar. The model here is an example that runs in the page; in the app it is your own, on your Mac or through Anthropic.

The arithmetic

Finish more, rest less. Snooze, rest more.

How long the check-up after a round lasts is not a setting you fiddle with. It is computed from how the round went, and asking to cut it short gets a polite “not yet”. Try it.

check-up = 1:15 × 2snoozes+1 ÷ (finished + 1)
  • Each snooze doubles it. More time now costs more time later, so you only take it when the round really needs it.
  • Each step you finish divides it. Clear three in a round and you are back in under a minute.
  • A long check-up counts as moving. Three minutes or more resets your seated time, which pushes the next movement break back.

The rest of the app

A plan you can see, and a clock you can always find.

Four more surfaces do the quiet work around the round: the graph and the grid for the plan, the menu bar and its cards for the time, and Settings for the numbers.

Strategy: the plan as a map.Your plan as a tree, one click from the round. The filled pill is Doing, the outlined one is Next. Dashed amber means a step is waiting on another, with an arrow from what it waits for. The model's suggestions appear as faint dashed ghosts until you accept them. Right-click any step for everything you can do to it; type a line in the quick-add field to add one. The board locks during check-up and the break.

Inside

Nine parts, and every hand-off between them, live.

The Monitor draws the app as it runs. A dot travels each time one part hands something to another: a request from B to the engine, a verdict from the policy, a tick to the engine, a patch to the store, a prompt to the model. A part that is working shows an arc; for a model turn, the arc fills against its deadline. In the round above it is wired to the session, so everything A and B do shows up on the Admin screen. A's Requests list every request with its three timestamps, the policy's default and the verdict, and the Event log keeps everything else.

In the app the Monitor is a developer tool, and the events are the app's own.

Your first ten minutes

From opening it to your first round.

The app lives in your menu bar. Click it for the window; right-click it for the menu. Nothing on the first screen needs a manual.

  1. Say what you're doing

    The first screen asks one thing: what would you like to do now? Type a line and press Return.

  2. Say what's next

    The same question again. Your second line becomes Next. The goal above them is named later, if ever.

  3. Ready? OK

    Add more steps if you like, then OK. That asks to open the session and start round one.

  4. Go

    “Ready when you are.” Press Go and the ring starts. Nothing else ever starts a round.

  5. Next phase

    Done early? Press Next phase to ask to move on, then Go. Not done? When time is up, the screen says so and waits.

  6. Connect your model

    Optional. Settings ⌘, → Model → Ollama, then type the name of a model you have pulled.

What it is, briefly

A menu-bar app with three parts: a plan you keep as a tree of steps, a session engine that runs the rounds by fixed rules, and an optional model consulted once per round.

DeterministicThe same choices give the same clock, every time.
LocalYour plan and its full history are saved on your Mac.
QuietFour short sounds, no streaks, no scores, no confetti.
YoursThe model proposes. Only you change the plan.
